Scorch

Scorch makes head-to-head running fair across different paces. It turns mapped local routes into scheduled races where each runner gets a pace-based start and the whole field races toward one finish.

The problem

Local competition is often club-dependent, pace-matched, or tied to formal race calendars.

My role

I defined the product, designed the athlete journey, built the iOS client and backend, and set the privacy, safety, and launch gates.

The key decision

I removed cash prizes, payments, cycling, health-data integrations, and broad public claims from the first release so the pilot can prove free, run-only fair-start racing.

What is real now

The complete free-race lifecycle is validated against the backend. Outdoor physical-device testing is the next gate.

Verified build evidence

As of July 2026, 256 automated tests and 118 release-readiness checks pass across registration, server-timed starts, route telemetry, evidence-backed finishes, review, and results.

HardBeet

Active development

Care should not require surveillance. HardBeet creates a respectful daily check-in agreement: one deliberate tap, people you approve, and a clear escalation path when a check-in is missed.

Product boundary

Only a deliberate tap creates the shared signal. No live location or private phone activity is exposed, and offline check-ins stay visibly unconfirmed until the backend receives them.

Build-readiness evidence: React Native client, Supabase backend, consent model, notification escalation, privacy controls, offline behavior, localization foundation, security hardening, and release QA. The current candidate has passed 29 automated tests plus backend, access-control, and account-deletion checks. Physical-device QA is the next gate before external distribution.

Aurora

Working prototype

Aurora turns cruise history, route preferences, cabin needs, and a price threshold into explainable fit recommendations and quiet price-watch opportunities.

Product boundary

Recommendations expose route fit, tradeoffs, and confidence before asking someone to watch or act. Unsupported or private data paths are blocked instead of quietly scraped.

Prototype evidence: Cruise Passport, itinerary-fit model, Decision Confidence layer, privacy boundaries, source-policy architecture, installable PWA, backend scaffolding, and native shells. The fixture-backed validation suite currently passes 25 of 25 tests. A compliant live-source adapter and observed planning sessions are the next gates.
